Privacy

Last updated 16 November 2026

I build small apps on my own. They are built to keep your work on your own device, and none of them is an advertising product. Some apps may collect anonymous usage and crash information so I can see how the app is performing and fix what is broken. This page explains what that can include and how to stop it.

What this covers

This is the privacy policy for the Android apps and web apps I publish, current and future, and for this website at www.labrarf.com. If you reached this page from an app's listing or from a link inside an app, this is that app's privacy policy.

These apps are made by Ray Labra, an independent developer working alone, and published under the name labrarf. I am responsible for the information described here, and the contact address at the bottom of this page reaches me directly.

Some apps publish a policy of their own with details specific to them. Where one does, that policy is linked from the app itself and from its store listing, and it governs for that app.

Your content stays on your device

Whatever you make or keep in an app, such as your notes, your saved work, your pictures, your settings and your progress, is stored on the device you use it on: in your browser's own storage for a web app, or in the app's private storage on Android. I do not run a server that receives it, and I never see it.

An app may offer to send your content somewhere you choose, for example to a file you export or to a cloud storage account you connect yourself. That only happens when you ask for it, and it goes to you, not to me.

Analytics and diagnostics

I may collect anonymous usage and diagnostic information from my apps and from this website, either now or in future releases, to understand how they are used, to measure performance and stability, and to decide what to build or fix next. Depending on the app and the tools in use, this can include:

  • app or page version, device model, operating system version, screen size, language and approximate region, usually derived from your IP address at the country or city level;
  • usage events, such as which screens or pages were opened, which features were used, how long a session lasted, and whether an action succeeded or failed;
  • crash reports and error diagnostics, which can include a technical log of what the app was doing at the moment it failed;
  • a randomly generated installation or device identifier, and in a web app a cookie or a similar storage key, used to tell one installation or browser from another and to count returning use.

This information is about how the software behaves, not about who you are. It is not tied to your name or email address, because my apps do not ask for them. I handle it in aggregate, and I do not try to identify you from it or combine it with anything else to do so.

Where analytics or crash reporting is used, it is normally provided by a third party acting on my behalf, and the information passes to that provider under its own privacy policy. An app that uses one will identify it, in the app or in a policy of its own. Separately, Google Play gives me aggregate installation, performance and crash statistics for any Android app, whether or not the app itself contains anything extra.

Where the law requires your consent for this, for example for non-essential cookies or similar technologies, the app or page asks you first and you can decline. An app that offers an in-app setting to turn analytics off will show it in its settings. You can also limit it in your own browser or device settings, for example through your device's privacy controls, your browser's cookie settings or a do-not-track setting where one is honored.

What I will not do

  • I do not sell your personal information, and I do not share it with data brokers.
  • I do not show ads in my apps, and I do not build advertising profiles about you.
  • I do not put the content you create into analytics, and I do not use it to train anything.
  • If an app ever needs something genuinely more, such as an account, a password or a payment, it will ask you plainly at the time and say what it is for. This page will never be the only notice you get.

Services these apps rely on

An app has to be delivered to you, and the companies that deliver it see the ordinary technical details of that delivery. That part is outside my control:

  • GitHub Pages hosts this website and the web apps. Like any web request, loading a page sends your IP address and browser details to GitHub's servers.
  • Google Fonts serves the typefaces used by this website and by some of the web apps hosted on it. Your browser fetches those font files from Google, which receives your IP address and browser details in the process. It receives nothing you do inside an app. An app that bundles its own fonts contacts no font service at all.
  • Google Play distributes the Android apps. Installing and updating an app happens through Google, under Google's privacy policy, not mine. If you use Android's own device backup, Android may include an app's local data in that backup, which you control in your device settings.
  • Formspree delivers messages sent through the contact form on this website. If you use that form, the name, email address and message you type are passed to Formspree and then emailed to me. I use them to reply to you and nothing else. If you do not use the form, Formspree receives nothing.

An individual app may use other services, such as a cloud storage provider you choose to connect. When it does, its own privacy policy names them.

Children

These apps are not directed at children under 13, and I do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has provided me with personal information, write to me and I will delete it.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your content lives on your device, so you remove it the way you remove anything else on your device:

  • Web app: clear that site's data in your browser settings.
  • Android app: clear the app's storage in Android settings, or uninstall it.

Export your work first if the app offers an export and you want to keep it. Uninstalling or clearing storage removes your content for good, and I have no copy to restore.

Usage and diagnostic information is kept only as long as it is useful for the purposes above, and in any case no longer than the retention period of the provider that collects it, after which it is deleted or kept only in aggregate form that can no longer be linked to a single installation. Because it carries no name or email address, I usually cannot find a particular person's records inside it.

You can ask me what I hold about you, ask for a copy, ask me to correct it, or ask me to delete it, by writing to the address below. Depending on where you live you may have further rights under laws such as the GDPR or the CCPA, including the right to object to this processing and the right to complain to your local data protection authority. I will respond within a month.

Changes

I may update this policy as the apps change, for example if an app begins collecting something it did not collect before. The date at the top changes with it, and a material change will be announced in the app or on this page rather than made quietly. Continuing to use an app after a change means the updated policy applies to that use.
