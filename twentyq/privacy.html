Twenty Q

Privacy

Last updated 2026-11-01

Twenty Q collects no personal data. It has no accounts, no ads and no analytics, and the game itself uses no network. The little it remembers stays on your own device.

What it stores

Three small things, and only on the device you play on:

  • Your game stats: how many games you have played, how many I won, how many times you stumped me, and the total turns across my wins.
  • Your sound setting: whether the beeps are on or off.
  • Your theme choice (web app only): light or dark.

On the web these live in your browser's own storage for this site. On Android they live in the app's private storage. None of it is sent anywhere, and I never see it. Your answers during a game are not saved at all. When a game ends, they are gone.

What it does not do

  • No accounts, sign-in, passwords or profiles.
  • No analytics, tracking pixels, advertising or crash reporting.
  • No selling or sharing of any data, because there is none to share.
  • No network use by the game. The knowledge base and the questions are built in, so it works offline.
  • The Android app does not ask for any permissions.

Where the web app comes from

The web app is a static page hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web page, loading it sends your IP address and browser details to GitHub's servers, which is outside my control. Its fonts are bundled with it, so no font service is contacted. Once it has loaded, it makes no further requests.

Deleting what it stores

  • Web: clear this site's data in your browser settings.
  • Android: clear the app's storage in Android settings, or uninstall it. If you use Android's own device backup, Android may include the app's stats in that backup, which is controlled in your device settings.

Children

Twenty Q does not ask for or collect anything about anyone, children included.

Changes

If this ever changes, I will update this page and its date. This page covers both the web app and the Android app.
