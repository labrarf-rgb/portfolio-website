Twenty Q

Game Web App Product Android
Open app

Twenty Q is a twenty questions game dressed up as a 90s handheld toy. You think of something, it asks questions, and it tries to guess what you picked within twenty.

I remember a round plastic gadget with a little green screen and four big buttons that somehow seemed to read your mind. I wanted that feeling back, and I wanted to run the project like a real product: decide what it is, decide what it is not, and keep playing it until it felt right.

Deciding what it is

The first plan was a tidy quiz app: a clean screen and a row of answer buttons. Once I saw a picture of the old toy next to it, I dropped that. The fun of the original was holding a little machine, so the app became one. It has a round shell, a dot matrix screen that types out its questions, chunky buttons and small beeps you can mute. I kept it an original design rather than copying the old toy's logo or shape, and coloured it in the warm, papery palette I use across my projects.

I cut things on purpose too. There is one mode, where the app does the guessing. There are no accounts and no ads, and it works offline, so you open it and play.

The rule I held the line on

An early build changed the buttons as the game went on, relabelling them for the opening question and dimming the ones that did not apply. It was easier to build that way, and it felt like cheating. The toy never did that. So the rule became that the four buttons never change: Unknown, No, Yes and Sometimes, every turn, with everything else on the little screen. A lot of later decisions came straight out of that one.

Playing it turned up the next rule. The opening walked through animal, vegetable and mineral as separate prompts but counted them as a single question, which made the count of twenty feel dishonest. Now each one counts, the way you would count them out loud.

Small things that took the most rounds

Most of my notes were about feel, not features. Long questions spilled off the screen, so every message the game can show now has to fit before anything ships. The toy looked lost in a big desktop window, so it grows to fill the space. On Android it started out off centre, and that got fixed too. I also asked how the knowledge compared with the original toy, which knew a couple of thousand things, and grew the list several times over so it has a fair chance with whatever you pick.

Why it is fun to try

The fun is watching it close in. Answer Sometimes when it honestly depends and Unknown when you have no idea; it can shrug off a wrong answer or two. If it misses at twenty it asks for five more, like the original did, and if you still beat it, it tells you what it was considering. Then you want to try something harder.

What it does

  • Plays like the toy. A round shell, a screen that types out each question, four big buttons and small synthesized beeps.
  • Four buttons that never change. All the guidance is on the screen, the way it was on the original.
  • Undo. Step back one turn, guesses included, and pick up exactly where you were.
  • Nothing to sign up for. It works offline, with no account and no ads.
  • Fits any screen. From a small phone to a large desktop window, in light or dark, and you can play with the keyboard (U, N, Y, S).
  • On Android too. The same game as a phone app. Android app coming to Google Play.
